Key takeaways:
  - Across all W and pi_S values, the outsource fraction changes much more
    with lambda and emergency-window width than with pi_S.
  - The outsourcing reason split shows how much of outsourcing is forced by
    infeasible insertion versus chosen economically by the objective.
  - Conditional on insertion being feasible, most emergencies are inserted
    once lambda reaches 1; the remaining outsourcing is the economic choice.
  - Higher pi_S mainly changes the cost composition by increasing the
    penalty attached to regular-request lateness; it does not overturn the
    insertion/outsourcing pattern.
  - C-class instances remain the most outsourcing-heavy; R-class instances
    remain the most insertion-friendly.
  - The main operational trade-off is still the trip-level completion delay:
    inserted emergencies delay the return of all specimens in that trip.
